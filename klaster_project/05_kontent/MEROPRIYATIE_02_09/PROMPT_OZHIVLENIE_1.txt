ПОСТ 1 — ОЖИВЛЕНИЕ КАДРА «Ворота открываются».
Runway Gen-4.5, режим image to video, первый кадр — готовая картинка по
PROMPT_KADR_1.txt. Длительность 10 секунд, соотношение 4:5, 24 кадра в секунду.
Gen-4.5 на подписке Max, платные кредиты не тратятся.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Animate the attached still as one continuous ten second shot with no cuts, no
transitions, no dissolves and no change of lens. The still is the first frame and
its composition is binding: what is drawn there stays where it is drawn, and the
motion is layered on top of it. This is a single breathing camera move through a
real place, in the language of a cinema title card for an industrial brand, not a
slideshow effect and not a parallax card.

THE CAMERA. One move, slow and mechanical, the way a dolly on a track behaves,
never handheld and never floaty. Over the full ten seconds the camera pushes
forward along its own axis by roughly two and a half metres, from eight metres in
front of the gate to five and a half metres, while rising by twelve centimetres
and losing one degree of its upward tilt. The push is eased at both ends: it
starts from a dead stop over the first fifteen frames, reaches constant speed by
second two, holds that speed through second eight, and settles to a stop over the
last twenty frames without overshoot or bounce. There is no roll, no yaw, no
whip, no orbit, no crash zoom, no rack focus to a different subject. Focal length
stays at 24 mm for the entire shot; do not zoom, and do not fake a zoom by
scaling the frame. Because the camera moves forward physically, the perspective
of the gate opening must genuinely change: the reveal of the concrete jamb on the
left opens a little wider, the far wall of the hall grows slightly, the dark
rectangle of the opening gains about eight percent of its height in frame. Motion
parallax between the foreground lettering, the gate plane and the far end of the
hall must be correct and consistent with a real 24 mm lens moving two and a half
metres, never a flat 2.5D card slide.

THE HEADLINE STAYS ABSOLUTELY STILL AND ABSOLUTELY SHARP. The standing aluminium
letters in the near foreground are physical objects bolted to the concrete, not
an overlay: they hold their exact position, their exact spelling and their exact
proportions for all ten seconds. As the camera pushes in they grow in frame the
way a real object two metres away grows when the camera closes by two and a half
metres, and their perspective opens accordingly — the side walls of the letters
on the left of the block become slightly more visible, the ones on the right
slightly less. They never wobble, never breathe, never warp, never re-render
themselves into different letterforms, never gain or lose a character, never drop
their chamfer, never turn amber on the faces. The Russian line reads exactly
«БРОКЕР-ТУР 2 СЕНТЯБРЯ» in frame one and reads exactly the same in frame two
hundred and forty. The supporting line beneath, «11:00 · 6-Я РАДИАЛЬНАЯ, 17 ·
КОНФЕРЕНЦ-ЗАЛ», is equally locked: same words, same spacing, same case, no
letter substitution, no Latin characters creeping in, no drift, no flicker, no
re-typesetting between frames. If any single character cannot be held stable,
hold the entire text block frozen rather than let it mutate. Nothing passes in
front of the characters at any point: dust, sparks and people all move behind
them or outside their bounding box.

THE BRAND MARK STAYS STILL. The milled steel gear-and-figure plate with the
wordmark «КЛАСТЕР» on the concrete wall left of the gate is a fixed object on a
fixed wall. It changes only by perspective as the camera advances, it keeps its
exact geometry — twelve teeth, clean circular centre, figure with arms raised to
both sides — and it never rotates, never pulses, never gains a glow, never
duplicates itself elsewhere in frame and never re-spells its wordmark. It does
not grow into a hero object: it stays a small maker's mark on a wall.

THE PEOPLE. The group of five in dark business outerwear inside the gate opening
walks forward toward the camera at an unhurried working pace, about 1.2 metres
per second, covering roughly twelve metres over the ten seconds so they finish
the shot much closer but still inside the threshold, never reaching the
foreground and never leaving frame. Their gait is natural and weighted: heel
strike, roll, push off, a slight vertical bob of three or four centimetres per
step, arms swinging out of phase with the legs, coats moving a beat behind the
bodies. They stay backlit silhouettes with warm rims the whole way; as they
approach, a little more detail resolves at the shoulders and collars but no face
ever becomes recognisable and no facial features are invented. One of them shifts
a rolled sheet of drawings from one arm to the other around second six. Another
glances up and to the right at the light shafts around second four, a small head
turn of no more than twenty degrees. Their contact shadows stretch and shorten
correctly as they move relative to the light behind them. Do not add people, do
not remove people, do not have anyone wave at the camera, and do not let the
group merge into a single blob.

THE DUST AND VAPOUR. The soft luminous wedge of construction dust and water
vapour rolling out of the hall is the main living element of the frame. It moves
slowly outward toward the camera at roughly 0.4 metres per second, turning over
itself in lazy vortices, thickening and thinning in waves with a period of about
three seconds so the shot never looks looped. Inside the four hard shafts of
morning light crossing the hall, individual motes resolve as small bright points
drifting and tumbling; outside the shafts the same volume is a soft grey haze.
The wedge must stay volumetric and behave like real airborne matter with mass:
never a flat animated gradient, never a moving texture pasted over the image,
never a symmetric particle spray. As the camera pushes in, the near edge of the
dust passes the lens plane at around second eight and the frame very slightly
softens in the lower third, then clears again.

THE SPARKS. The welder's cone of sparks off frame left continues in short bursts,
not as a constant stream: a burst of about one and a half seconds, then two
seconds of near darkness with only the amber glow of the cooling metal, then
another burst. During a burst the sparks arc down and to the left at about twelve
metres per second, the ones close to the source frozen as hard white hot points
and the ones further out stretched into short amber traces that fade over about
half a metre. A few land on the wet concrete and die on contact with a tiny puff
of steam. The light of each burst spills onto the left concrete jamb and the
nearest letter side walls, lifting them for the duration of the burst and falling
away again. Sparks never cross the headline block and never reach the group of
people.

THE WATER. The three thin threads of water still running off the lower edge of
the shutter continue to fall, break on the sill and scatter. The standing water on
the apron holds its mirror: as the camera advances, the reflections of the
letters, of the gate opening and of the amber practical inside the hall slide
across the surface correctly, in the opposite direction to the camera move. Every
few seconds a single drop from the shutter hits a puddle and sends out one clean
concentric ring that expands and dies. Do not add rain, do not add wind ripples
across the whole surface, do not turn the apron into a mirror-flat CG plane.

THE TRUCK. At the far right edge, the box truck continues backing in very slowly,
about 0.5 metres per second, its two reversing lamps holding steady and the red
tail light drawing its short smear along the wet concrete. It never enters the
central third of the frame and never becomes the subject. Around second seven a
faint amber turn indicator blinks twice at the extreme edge and stops.

THE LIGHT. Global lighting is stable throughout: the 5600 K open sky key from
behind camera, the 6500 K rim from the far doorway deep in the hall, the 2800 K
sodium practical glowing on the interior wall with a soft bloom. The only light
that changes is the welder's burst, and the only global change is a very slow,
almost imperceptible lift of the hall interior over the ten seconds — as if the
morning is genuinely arriving — amounting to no more than a third of a stop in
the deep background and nothing at all in the foreground. Do not flicker the
practical, do not pulse the exposure, do not introduce a light that is not in the
first frame, do not change colour temperature anywhere, do not add lens flares
that were not there, and do not animate the anamorphic flare into a sweep.

GRADE AND TEXTURE. Grade, contrast, saturation and black point are identical in
every frame: black point at 6 of 255, rich contrasty film response, fine grain in
the shadows. The grain must be animated at 24 frames per second like real film
grain, never frozen as a static overlay and never boiling. Motion blur is
physically motivated at a 180 degree shutter: the walking figures and the falling
sparks carry it, the letters and the wall-mounted mark do not, because they are
not moving relative to the sensor beyond the camera's own advance.

WHAT MUST NOT HAPPEN, stated plainly because these are the failure modes that
kill this kind of shot. No morphing of any surface into another. No warping or
rippling of the concrete, the shutter slats, the columns or the ground plane. No
letters or numbers appearing, disappearing or changing anywhere in frame,
including on the truck, the walls, the floor marking and the hazard chevron. No
new signage, no invented Latin text, no watermark, no timestamp, no subtitles, no
user interface elements, no arrows, no progress bars. No cut, no fade in, no fade
out, no speed ramp, no slow motion, no time lapse. No camera shake, no
stabilisation wobble, no rolling shutter skew. No cartoon or painterly styling, no
neon grading, no orange and teal push. No extra light beams, no god rays beyond
the four shafts already present in the first frame, no floating embers filling the
whole frame. Nobody looks at the camera. The gate does not move: it has already
finished rising and stays at the top of its travel for the whole shot. The
building does not change: no added floors, no glass, no dark perforated cladding.

THE FEELING. Ten seconds of a real morning at a working industrial park, seen
from the outside looking in, with the gate already open and people walking out of
the dark toward the light while the dust turns in the shafts behind them. The
frame should feel patient and heavy and expensive, the way the opening shot of a
film feels, and it should end on the same clean readable headline it started on.
